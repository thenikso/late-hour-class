Sample lesson

Good guesses

How to estimate a number nobody told you.

Opening

Welcome everyone. Today: estimating numbers we can't look up.

Warm-up

How do you feel about guessing a number you don't know?

Stand on the line, anywhere between the two ends.

Warm-up

Open the line question. Ask one person from each end why they stand there.

Your turn · A

“There are about a billion grains of sand on this beach.” Best first move?

  • Agree
  • Disagree
  • Break it into parts
Break it into parts. How big is the beach? How many grains in a handful? Those you can guess.

A billion grains

Expected: break it into parts. Accept "disagree" only if they then estimate.

Explanation

Round boldly, multiply, then check the size

Use round numbers: 1, 2, 5, 10, 100. Being within a factor of ten is a good start.

An estimate is only as good as its weakest part.

Round boldly

Press E for the second line after asking which part they would trust least.

Your turn · D

Sensible or way off?

Sleep 8 hours · a 40 m car · a 2 litre bathtub · 20 slices in a loaf

Sensible, way off, way off, and the bread depends on the loaf.

Sensible or way off

The bread row accepts both answers: talk about it.

Your turn · F

How many litres of water do you drink in a year?

Mark each part: per day, days, result.

About 2 litres × about 365 days ≈ 700 litres.

Water in a year

Anything from a few hundred to a thousand litres is fine if the parts are shown.

Your turn · G

Pizzas at school in a week: which part would you check first?

800 students × ½ buy lunch × 5 days × 2 slices ÷ 8

The share who buy lunch. It's the least certain guess, and the total moves with it.

The weakest part

The number of students is easy to look up; the share is a pure guess.

Your turn · M

10,000 pizzas a week. Believable?

That's about 12 pizzas per student per week: far too many. Something went wrong.

Check the size

Look for a comparison with something known, not a recalculation.
